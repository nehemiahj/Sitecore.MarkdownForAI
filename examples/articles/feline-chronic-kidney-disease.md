# Chronic Kidney Disease in Cats: Care and Diet Guidelines

> [!NOTE]
> **In short:** Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a slow loss of kidney function that is **common in older cats**. It cannot be cured, but **diet, hydration and regular check-ups** can help many cats live comfortably for months to years after diagnosis.

## 🩺 What chronic kidney disease is

The kidneys **filter waste from the blood**, **balance water and minerals**, help **control blood pressure** and make a hormone that tells the body to **produce red blood cells**.

In CKD, kidney tissue is slowly replaced by scar tissue. The kidneys can lose a large share of their function before signs appear, which is why **routine blood and urine tests matter for cats over 7**.

- **Chronic** means it develops over months or years. It is different from **acute kidney injury**, which happens suddenly (for example after eating lilies) and is an emergency.
- **Progressive** means it usually gets slowly worse over time. How fast varies a lot between cats.
- **Manageable** means treatment aims to slow it down and keep your cat comfortable.

---

## 👀 Signs to watch for

Early CKD often has **no visible signs**. As it progresses, you may notice:

- **Drinking more** than usual, or waiting by taps and water bowls
- **Peeing more**, with bigger clumps in the litter box
- **Eating less** or becoming fussy about food
- **Weight loss** and loss of muscle along the back
- **Vomiting** or drooling, sometimes with nausea before meals
- **A dull, unkempt coat**
- **Bad breath** with an ammonia smell
- **Constipation**, because the body is short of water
- **Low energy** and sleeping more
- **Weak neck muscles**, with the head held low (a sign of low potassium)
- **Sudden blindness** or dilated pupils (a sign of high blood pressure)

## 🚨 When to call your vet

> [!CAUTION]
> **Go to an emergency vet now** if your cat:
> - **may have touched or eaten any part of a lily**, drunk lily vase water, or licked antifreeze
> - **has not peed in 24 hours** or strains without passing urine
> - **is vomiting repeatedly**, collapses, has a seizure or suddenly cannot see

> [!WARNING]
> **Book a visit within a day or two** if your cat:
> - **stops eating for more than 24 hours**. Cats that do not eat can develop serious liver problems quickly.
> - **loses weight** you can see or feel
> - **drinks or pees noticeably more** than before

## 🔬 Diagnosis and staging

Your vet will usually check:

- **Blood tests:** creatinine and SDMA (markers of kidney filtering), plus phosphorus, potassium and red blood cell count
- **Urine tests:** how concentrated the urine is (urine specific gravity), protein loss (UPC ratio) and signs of infection
- **Blood pressure:** high blood pressure is common with CKD and can damage the eyes, heart and brain
- **Imaging:** ultrasound or X-rays in some cats, to look at kidney size and shape or rule out stones

### IRIS stages

Vets use the **International Renal Interest Society (IRIS)** stages to describe how advanced CKD is and to guide treatment. The stage is based on blood results taken when the cat is **stable and well hydrated**.

| IRIS stage | Blood creatinine (mg/dL) | Blood SDMA (µg/dL) | What it usually means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Under 1.6 | Under 18 | Kidney changes found, normal filtering. Often no signs. |
| **2** | 1.6 to 2.8 | 18 to 25 | Mild loss of function. Signs are mild or absent. |
| **3** | 2.9 to 5.0 | 26 to 38 | Moderate loss. Signs such as weight loss and poor appetite are common. |
| **4** | Over 5.0 | Over 38 | Severe loss. Higher risk of illness from waste build-up. |

Each stage is also **sub-staged** by:

| Sub-stage | Measured by | Cat ranges |
|---|---|---|
| **Protein in urine** | Urine protein to creatinine ratio (UPC) | Under 0.2 normal · 0.2 to 0.4 borderline · Over 0.4 high |
| **Blood pressure** | Systolic pressure (mmHg) | Under 140 normal · 140 to 159 borderline · 160 to 179 high · 180 or more severe |

> [!TIP]
> Ask your vet for **a copy of each set of results**. Tracking creatinine, SDMA, phosphorus and weight over time shows whether treatment is working.

## 🍽️ Diet

Diet is **the treatment with the strongest evidence** for cats with CKD from stage 2 onwards. Prescription kidney (renal) diets are designed to reduce the work the kidneys have to do.

### Kidney diet compared with regular adult cat food

| Nutrient | Kidney (renal) diet | Typical adult maintenance food | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phosphorus** | **Restricted** | Higher | High phosphorus speeds up kidney damage. This is the most important change. |
| **Protein** | **Moderate, high quality** | Higher | Less waste for the kidneys to clear, while still protecting muscle. |
| **Omega-3 fatty acids** | **Added** | Variable | May reduce inflammation in the kidneys. |
| **Potassium** | **Added** | Standard | Cats with CKD often lose potassium in their urine. |
| **Sodium** | **Controlled** | Variable | Helps with blood pressure. |
| **B vitamins** | **Added** | Standard | Lost in the larger volume of urine. |
| **Calories** | **Higher** | Standard | Helps cats with small appetites keep weight on. |

### Phosphorus targets

Your vet will aim to keep blood phosphorus in a target range for your cat's stage:

| IRIS stage | Target blood phosphorus |
|---|---|
| **2** | 2.7 to 4.6 mg/dL |
| **3** | Under 5.0 mg/dL |
| **4** | Under 6.0 mg/dL |

If diet alone does not reach the target, your vet may add a **phosphate binder**, a powder mixed into food that stops phosphorus being absorbed.

### Switching foods

1. **Ask your vet first.** A kidney diet is not right for every cat or every stage.
2. **Switch slowly over 1 to 3 weeks**, mixing more of the new food in each day.
3. **Never force-feed** or switch while your cat is feeling sick. Cats can develop a lasting dislike of food eaten while nauseous.
4. **Try different textures and flavors.** Most kidney diets come in several wet and dry versions.
5. **Eating something is better than eating nothing.** If your cat refuses every kidney diet, talk to your vet about other options.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Avoid adding** treats, supplements or home-cooked food without your vet's advice. Many treats and "natural" supplements are high in phosphorus or salt.

## 💊 Treatment

Treatment depends on the stage and on your cat's specific problems. Your vet may recommend:

| Problem | Common approaches | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Dehydration** | Wet food, water fountains, fluids under the skin (subcutaneous fluids) | Many owners learn to give fluids at home. Dose and frequency are set by your vet. |
| **High phosphorus** | Kidney diet, phosphate binder | Recheck blood phosphorus after changes. |
| **Low potassium** | Potassium supplement | Given by mouth. |
| **High blood pressure** | Blood pressure medication | Protects the eyes, heart, brain and kidneys. |
| **Protein in urine** | Medication that reduces protein loss | Usually rechecked with a UPC test. |
| **Nausea and poor appetite** | Anti-nausea medication, appetite stimulants | Some appetite stimulants come as a gel rubbed on the ear. |
| **Anemia** | Hormone injections, iron | Used when red blood cell counts fall too low. |

> [!NOTE]
> **Doses are not listed here on purpose.** Only give medication prescribed for your cat, at the dose your vet set. **Never give human pain relievers.**

## 🏠 Home care

### Hydration

- **Feed wet food** where possible. It contains about 70 to 80% water.
- **Offer several water bowls** around the home, away from the litter box and food.
- **Try a water fountain.** Many cats prefer moving water.
- **Add water** to wet food, a spoon at a time.

### Monitoring

| What to track | How | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight** | Weigh weekly on the same scale | Gradual weight loss is an early warning sign. |
| **Appetite** | Note how much food is left each day | Falling appetite often means nausea or worsening CKD. |
| **Thirst and urine** | Check litter clump size and number | Sudden changes need a vet check. |
| **Behavior** | Note hiding, sleeping more or vomiting | Helps your vet adjust treatment. |

### Check-up schedule

Your vet will set the schedule, but a common pattern is:

- **Stable stage 1 or 2:** every 6 months
- **Stage 3 or 4:** every 1 to 3 months
- **After any medication or diet change:** a recheck within 2 to 4 weeks

## ⚠️ Household dangers for the kidneys

| Hazard | Where it is found | Why it is dangerous | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lilies** (true lilies and daylilies) | Bouquets, gardens, pollen, vase water | Even a small amount can cause sudden kidney failure in cats | **Keep them out of the home.** Go to an emergency vet immediately after any contact. |
| **Antifreeze** (ethylene glycol) | Garages, driveways, some de-icers | Causes sudden kidney failure; it tastes sweet | Store sealed, clean spills at once, emergency vet immediately after any exposure. |
| **Human pain relievers** (NSAIDs such as ibuprofen and naproxen) | Medicine cabinets | Can cause kidney damage and stomach ulcers | Never give them. Keep medicines shut away. |
| **Vitamin D rat and mouse poisons** (cholecalciferol) | Pest control baits | Raise blood calcium and damage the kidneys | Use pet-safe pest control. Emergency vet if eaten. |

## ❓ Common questions

**Can chronic kidney disease be cured?**
No. Damaged kidney tissue does not grow back. Treatment aims to **slow progression and keep your cat comfortable**.

**How long will my cat live?**
It varies widely. Cats diagnosed early and managed well often live **for years**. Your vet can give a better estimate based on your cat's stage, protein loss and blood pressure.

**Is my cat in pain?**
CKD itself is not usually painful, but **nausea, dehydration and constipation** cause discomfort. Treating these makes a big difference.

**Can I give subcutaneous fluids at home?**
Many owners can. **Ask your vet clinic to teach you**, and give only the amount and schedule your vet sets.

---

## ✅ Key takeaways

- **CKD is common in senior cats** and is often found on routine blood tests before signs appear.
- **IRIS staging** (1 to 4) guides treatment and check-up frequency.
- **A kidney diet with restricted phosphorus** is the most effective treatment from stage 2 onwards.
- **Hydration** matters every day: wet food, several bowls and, if prescribed, fluids under the skin.
- **Lilies and antifreeze are emergencies.** Do not wait for signs.
